How SAFE / WATCH / OVER is worked out

For every pay entry the app does the same four steps

  1. Start from net pay for the period (after tax and National Insurance — what reached your bank).
  2. Take off allowed deductions. Care costs that enable you to work (paying someone other than a close relative to look after the person you care for, or your children) and pension contributions are each deducted up to a cap of half of the period's net pay — whichever is lower, the cap or what you entered. Work equipment or specialist clothing, travel for work and business costs are deducted in full.
  3. Convert to a weekly figure. Weekly pay counts as it is; four-weekly pay ÷ 4; monthly pay × 12 ÷ 52.
  4. Compare with the weekly earnings limit. Under 90 % of the limit = SAFE; between 90 % and the limit = WATCH; at or over = OVER LIMIT. The app also tells you roughly how much room is left.
I have two jobs — which one is tested?

Neither on its own: the limit applies to your total earnings. With Premium you can add more than one income source; the Dashboard adds each source's latest weekly figure together and gives the verdict on the combined amount, with a per-source breakdown underneath. On the free plan the app tracks one job, so if you have a second income the single-job verdict is not the whole picture.

Does the app know about the Carer's Allowance rate or other benefits?

It shows the weekly rate for information only. It does not calculate Universal Credit, Carer's Element, overlapping benefits or anything else, and it cannot tell you whether you are entitled to Carer's Allowance. It only tests earnings against the limit.

Not a DWP tool, not advice. The app applies the published earnings rule as we understand it to figures you type. A WATCH or OVER result is a prompt to check with the Carer's Allowance Unit, Citizens Advice or a welfare rights adviser before you rely on it — and a SAFE result does not replace reporting a change of earnings to DWP. The app is not affiliated with the Department for Work and Pensions or Carers UK.

Where the limit, rate and caps come from

The weekly earnings limit, the weekly allowance rate and the two deduction caps are not hard-coded. They sit in a small dated rules file: each value carries the date it took effect, the official gov.uk page it came from and the date we last checked it. The app downloads a fresh copy of that file (an 8-second timeout, then it falls back to the last copy it saved, then to the copy built into the app). The verdict never waits for the network, and a malformed or impossible download is rejected rather than used.

The limit went up in April — are my old entries recalculated?

No, deliberately. Every entry stores the limit it was judged against at the time, together with the deductions applied, the weekly figure and the verdict. An uprating changes verdicts for new entries only, so your history remains an honest record of what was true in each period.

Can I see which limit an entry used?

Yes — open the entry from History; the limit in force and the breakdown are shown on the detail screen.

"Is your pay the same amount every payday?"

The first screen asks this because the weekly-conversion rule above is only right for regular pay. If your amount changes from payslip to payslip (variable hours, shifts, zero-hours), DWP averages earnings over a period, and this version of the app cannot do that calculation. Rather than show a SAFE that might be wrong, the app sends fluctuating-pay carers to a screen that says so. Anything you had already saved is kept. If you chose "fluctuating" by mistake, tap "Actually, my pay is regular" on that screen.

History, What-If and export

Why can't I edit an entry?

Entries are immutable once saved so the history is evidence-grade: you can delete an entry and add a corrected one, but never change what was recorded. The free plan shows your last four entries; Premium shows all of them.

What-If

From the Dashboard, try a different pay, frequency or deductions before a pay rise or a change of hours; the same engine gives the verdict instantly. On the free plan it is a scratchpad — "Preview only, not saved to your history". Premium lets you save several scenarios and compare them side by side.

PDF export (Premium)

Choose a date range and the app generates a PDF of those pay periods — entries, deductions, weekly figures, verdicts and the limit used — with the same disclaimer the app shows on screen. It goes out through the iOS print dialog or the share sheet, to Citizens Advice, an adviser or your own files; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Payday reminder (Premium)

A local notification on your payday, following your pay frequency, nudging you to log the payslip. Dates are calculated by calendar day, so the clocks changing never moves it.

Restore and cancel

Restore on a new phone

Settings → Restore purchase. You will see "Restore requested…" and, if the Apple ID on this phone holds an active subscription, Premium unlocks within a moment. If nothing happens, check you are signed in to the App Store with the Apple ID that bought it.

Cancel

Through your Apple ID, not in the app: Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions → Carer's Allowance Tracker, or apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions, at least 24 hours before renewal. Premium lasts to the end of the period already paid; afterwards you keep the free features and all your data stays on the phone.

Refund

Apple takes the payment, so a refund is requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com. We cannot see your transaction.

Privacy

Your pay entries, deductions, income sources and settings are stored on your phone only. There is no account, no server of ours, no analytics and no advertising. Two things touch the network and neither carries anything about you: the rules file described above, downloaded from a static page we host on GitHub Pages, and the App Store checking your subscription. The payday reminder is a local notification. A PDF leaves the phone only to the destination you choose in the share sheet. Deleting the app deletes all of its data; your own iPhone backup may hold a copy. Full text: privacy policy.
