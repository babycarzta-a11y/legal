Questions people ask

Why is my payment positive when I expected a negative number?

Money has a direction. The iPhone number pad has no minus key, so each money field carries an In / Out chip instead of a sign: In is money you receive, Out is money you pay. Typing a minus flips the chip; the ± key on the keyboard bar does the same. A loan you take: PV In, PMT Out. Savings: deposits Out, the final balance In. The result line says "you pay" or "you receive" in words.

"All amounts have the same sign" — what does that mean?

Solving for a rate or a term needs money going both ways. If PV, PMT and FV are all In (or all Out), there is no rate that balances them. Flip the chip on the amounts you pay.

The app says "2 IRRs". Which one is right?

When cash flows change sign more than once, the NPV curve can cross zero more than once, and every crossing is a mathematically valid IRR. The app scans the whole range from −99 % to +1,000 % per period, reports every root and the number of sign changes. Decide with the NPV at your required rate, or use MIRR, which assumes a finance rate and a reinvestment rate and always has one answer. The TVM worksheet does the same for I/Y: it returns the root nearest zero and lists the others.

What is the difference between P/Y and C/Y?

P/Y is how many payments there are per year; C/Y is how often interest is compounded. A Canadian mortgage, for instance, has monthly payments (P/Y 12) with semi-annual compounding (C/Y 2). Leave them equal for the usual case. I/Y is always the annual rate in percent.

My amortization totals differ from the bank's by a few cents.

Check Round schedules to cents in Settings. When it is on, interest and the payment are rounded to the cent every period, as a lender's statement does, and the final payment absorbs the difference. When it is off, every period is carried at full precision and the totals can differ by cents from a statement. The payment is calculated if you leave the field empty; extra payments per period and a one-time lump sum shorten the loan and the summary shows the interest and time saved.

Which bond conventions are used?

Prices are per 100 of face value and clean (without accrued interest); the dirty price is shown separately. Coupon dates are counted back from maturity with the end-of-month rule, as spreadsheets do. Day count is Actual/Actual or 30/360 (US/NASD with the February rules); the default follows your region and can be changed in Settings. For yield to call, enter the call date as the maturity and the call price as the redemption value.

Margin or markup?

Margin is profit as a share of the selling price; markup is profit as a share of cost. A 25 % markup is a 20 % margin. Enter any two of cost, price, margin and markup. French users: the app follows the French retail convention (taux de marque / taux de marge).

How does percent change become CAGR?

With one period the worksheet gives the simple percent change. With several periods it gives the average growth per period, so 100 growing to 121 over 2 years is 10 % a year.

How many decimals does it calculate with?

About 35 significant digits internally, always. Settings → Decimal places only changes how many are shown. A solved value keeps its full precision until you edit the field, as a hardware calculator does.

What is the difference between History and Saved?

History keeps your last 100 solved calculations automatically, newest first, and is free. Saved (Pro) is where you keep a calculation on purpose with a name and a note ("Bank offer 4.9 %") and come back to it. Both live in a file on the iPhone and are included in Back up to a file; importing a backup adds and never deletes, and the history always merges.

Can I get a schedule into Excel?

With Pro: CSV table exports the schedule or table; PDF report exports inputs, results, the steps and the schedule. Numbers and dates in the CSV follow your region's format. The free version shares any result as plain text.

Error messages, decoded

"The payment does not even cover the interest, so the balance is never repaid."PMT is smaller than one period's interest on PV. Increase the payment, shorten nothing: N cannot be solved for a loan that grows.
"No IRR: the NPV does not reach zero between −99 % and 1,000 % per period."The flows never break even at any sensible rate, for example a project whose inflows are smaller than the outlay. Check signs and amounts.
"Cash flows need at least one negative and one positive amount."Mark the investment as Out (negative) and the returns as In.
"The settlement date must be before the maturity date."Swap the two dates in the bond worksheet.
"The salvage value cannot be higher than the cost."Depreciation would be negative; correct one of the two.
"That is more than 12,000 periods."A term this long is almost always a units mistake, for example years entered where months were meant.
"The App Store isn't available right now."No connection, signed out of the App Store, or in-app purchases blocked by Screen Time. Fix one and open the paywall again.
